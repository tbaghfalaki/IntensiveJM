# Dynamic Prediction in Intensive Longitudinal Studies
Intensive longitudinal studies, characterized by high-frequency repeated measurements within individuals, offer rich information on dynamic processes but pose substantial methodological and computational challenges for estimation and prediction. In this paper, we review and systematically compare several statistical approaches for parameter estimation and dynamic prediction, including joint modeling, landmarking, two-stage methods, and MFPCCox, a flexible framework that combines multivariate functional principal component analysis with a Cox proportional hazards model to accommodate high-dimensional longitudinal markers. The comparison covers both linear and nonlinear longitudinal trajectories, as well as settings with univariate and multivariate markers. Through extensive simulation studies, we evaluate the predictive accuracy, robustness, and computational performance of these methods under a range of data-generating mechanisms. The proposed approaches are further illustrated using a clinical dataset of patients with cerebral vasospasm following subarachnoid hemorrhage, demonstrating their practical relevance and applicability in real-world intensive longitudinal settings.
